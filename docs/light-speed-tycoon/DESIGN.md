# Light Speed Tycoon — Design

A fun-first city-builder/tycoon where you are a fiber builder and provider. The town grows on its own; you build the network, sell service, and compete. Real fiber concepts, simplified for play.

## Pillars

1. **Build it right, profit for decades.** Glass you lay early pays off through every tech upgrade.
2. **Real flavor, not a textbook.** OLTs, splitters, make-ready, take rate, CPGA, MEF circuits — all present, all simplified.
3. **Every layer matters.** Access, core, and transport each create their own capacity and reliability puzzles.

## Network model

```
Exchange (metro IX)
   │  transport: lease a wave · buy an IRU · build your own route   [Region view]
   ▼
CO site ── core router ── DWDM add/drop
   │  F1 feeder
   ▼
Hub / OLT (PON ports)
   │  F2 distribution, along streets (aerial or underground)
   ▼
Cabinet (splitters, e.g. 1:32)
   │  drop (auto on signup)
   ▼
Home · MDU demarc + building router · SMB
```

- **CO siting:** pick and acquire a CO location per town; cost and constraints vary by site.
- **Transport:** connects a CO to the exchange or to another of your COs. Enables city-to-city expansion and core topology (rings).
- **Capacity chain:** PON port → OLT uplink → core router ports → DWDM waves → transport → IX port. The tightest link is the bottleneck.
- **Dedicated (M3):** DIA, EPL, EVPL, E-Access/ENNI to carriers, waves, dark fiber. Customers include businesses, cell towers, schools, carriers. Won deals carry SLA exposure.

## Economy

- **Revenue:** residential/SMB MRC, MDU bulk, dedicated MRC + NRC, (later) wholesale.
- **Costs:** construction (per ft by method), drops/ONTs, equipment, payroll, pole rent, transport leases, IX ports, marketing, interest, SLA credits.
- **Monthly report:** subs, gross adds, churn, ARPU by product, CPGA, take rate by area, cost per passing vs target, cost per connection, EBITDA, cash, debt covenants.
- **Cost per passing (CPP):** outside-plant build cost (cable, cabinets, splitters) ÷ homes passed; drops/ONTs/install are cost per connection, tracked separately. A tunable target (~$1,000) colors every cable quote and cabinet preview (green ≤ target, amber ≤ 1.5×, red above) and anchors milestones. Lenders price debt off lifetime CPP: efficient builds get cheaper money (see Financing).
- **CO capacity:** each 1:32 splitter consumes one OLT PON port (start: 16 ports = 512 subs). Upgrades at the CO add OLT line cards (+16 ports) and later a second chassis, each with a cost.
- **Cost pressure:** per-pole make-ready on aerial, engineering/permit fees per order, a monthly overhead that grows with the company (a small base, per customer with the support level, per cabinet and route-mile: round 4c/4d), and a lean starting purse so the starting cash covers only ~25–33% of the town; tuned by headless simulation.
- **Cabinet coverage:** a cabinet serves homes within a street-distance reach (800 ft, tuned so placement matters); splitter ports (32 each) cap subscribers. Placement preview shades the reach and counts homes covered.
- **Competitor response:** incumbents run promos in neighborhoods you enter (ARPU compression); rival overbuilder races for unbuilt areas and RFQs.

## Technical defaults (Claude's call, revisit if needed)

- **Stack:** Vite + TypeScript + PixiJS; Vitest for the sim; Playwright for UI checks.
- **Architecture:** pure-TS simulation core with no rendering dependencies, run on a fixed daily tick; renderer and UI read sim state. Keeps the sim unit-testable and deterministic.
- **Determinism:** seeded RNG for map generation and events, so a seed reproduces a game.
- **City representation:** street graph (intersections + segments) over a tile grid; parcels attach to segments; cable is placed on segments.
- **Saves:** localStorage autosave + JSON export/import.

## Financing (round 3, settled)

Owner decisions of 2026-10-20 (products, ARR underwriting, risk pricing, escalating covenants, default as game over), with the details the implementation and tuning settled. Values are in the balance table below.

- **Products.** A revolving **credit line**: draw and repay any time; interest on each day's end-of-day balance (actual/365), charged at the month close; the limit is 0.5 × ARR and grows with the business. **Term loans** for builds: a fixed amount over 5 or 7 years, level monthly payments of principal and interest with exact whole-dollar amortization (the rounding lands on the last payment); the level payment is always the one that repays the loan at the rate charged, so it is re-set over the months left whenever the penalty below starts or stops and after a missed payment (the surcharge is paid as it accrues, never as a lump at the end); the first close after origination charges only the stub interest; prepay any part at any time with the interest accrued since the 1st (the payment is re-amortized over the months left). Term capacity is 3 × ARR less the term balances owed; the smallest loan is $10,000.
- **Underwriting on recurring revenue.** ARR is the revenue of the last 3 closed months × 4, so there is nothing to borrow before the first subscriber pays and very little for the first months (early tension). The lender's cash flow is max(annualized trailing operating cash flow, 35% of ARR): the **cash-flow floor** judges a young network on its recurring revenue while its operating cash flow is ≤ 0 or small; with no revenue and no positive cash flow, debt is unmeasurable (a breach). **Leverage** = total debt ÷ that cash flow; **debt-service coverage** = that cash flow ÷ the debt service scheduled over the next 12 months (each loan's payments, plus a year of interest on the line's balance).
- **Risk pricing.** Rate = base 5% + leverage spread (≤ 2×: none, ≤ 3×: +0.75%, ≤ 4×: +1.5%, ≤ 5×: +2.5%, above or unmeasurable: +4%) + **CPP spread** on lifetime cost per passing (green: none, amber: +1%, red: +2.5%; none before anything is passed) + product (credit line +1%, 7-year loan +0.5%). The quotes show each part, with a plain-language CPP line ("Cost per passing $742 is under target: no CPP spread").
- **Fixed vs floating.** A term loan's rate is fixed at origination, priced on pro forma leverage (with the new loan, to the hundredth as the quote shows it). The credit line floats: it is re-priced at every close on that close's leverage (to the hundredth, as the report shows it) and the ended month's cost per passing.
- **Covenants with escalation and cure.** Tested at every close after the debt service: leverage ≤ 5× and coverage ≥ 1.2× (when anything is scheduled), both to the hundredth as the Bank and the report show them (5.00× passes); the debt service counts each loan's real payments over the next 12 months, its last one included. A breach steps the stage up: **warning** (a notice, nothing else) → **penalty** (+3% on every balance) → **frozen** (no draws and no new term loans; repaying and prepaying still work; the penalty stays). Two compliant closes in a row step it down one stage (cure); repaying all debt clears it at the next close, whatever the stage (until then a frozen line stays frozen, and says so). The bank will still write a loan whose quote says it would breach (capacity, 3.5 × ARR with the line, is about twice what the covenants allow on the floor): the quote warns, and that is the reckless player's path.
- **Missed payment.** Debt service comes out of the cash left after the month's revenue and running costs. If that is short, nothing is paid: the unpaid interest is added to what is owed, the schedule slips a month (the payments are re-set to repay the larger balance over it), a **late fee** of max($1,000, 5% of what was due) is charged, and the line jumps straight to frozen.
- **Default.** A payment missed while frozen ends the game (game-over reason "default", the same machinery as insolvency); the insolvency check runs after the debt review. A **reminder** notice is posted 5 days before a close when the cash projected at the close (today's cash plus the month's billing, less its overhead, pole rent, marketing, support and installs at the month's pace) is below the estimated debt service; the Bank shows the same projection beside the next payment. With the line frozen and debt owed, the insolvency warning and the HUD say that the next close in the red is a default (no grace closes are left).
- **Accounting.** Interest (late fees included), principal and loan proceeds are ledger categories: operating cash flow excludes them (it is what the covenants measure), net cash flow includes them, and cost per passing is untouched by debt.

## Round 4: briefing, loan polish, pricing realism, aerial vs underground (settled)

Owner decisions of 2026-10-20 (the month-end briefing is the owner's idea), with the shapes the round-4 contract fixed (`types.ts`, schemaVersion 4). Values are in `balance.ts` (its Round 4 section, plus the round-2 values the round-4 tuning moved); the tables below say where they landed and why.

- **Month-end briefing.** At a month close the game pauses and a sheet shows the period at a glance (net, cash, subscribers, passings, take rate, cost per passing vs target, debt and covenant status), what changed against the period before, the period's notices, the revenue and cost breakdown, and a forward look (next payment, cash projected after the next close's payment and, while frozen, its sweep, runway, the frozen-line countdown). Continue resumes the speed the player had. A per-browser setting (not game state): every month (default), every quarter (a quarter's briefing sums its three months against the three before) or only when something needs attention (urgent advice or a notice that needs action; a storm or dig-in only while plant is still out at the close, a full cabinet, a full OLT or paused installs only while still so at the close). Off in headless play and, unless asked, in tests (`?debug=1` pages). The data is `advisor.buildBriefing`, pure; the period's notices come from a cursor each round-4 close records (`MonthlyReport.ops.lastNoticeId`).
- **Advisor.** 1-3 ranked suggestions from deterministic rules in the pure sim (`advisor.advise`), each with a reason built from the figures and an action the UI performs: move the camera to a cabinet, corner or street, or open Pricing, the Bank or the OLT panel. Rules never advise what would backfire: cures and financing are verified pro forma (`debt.cureFor` over `debt.cureOptions`, `debt.fundingHint`), and once the bank has called the loans, or would call them before a cure could lift the freeze, the only cure offered is repaying everything (when cash covers it).
- **Frozen line.** While frozen, each close sweeps half of the month's positive operating cash flow (never cash below a small reserve) to repay the line, then term loans. After 6 frozen closes in a row the bank calls the loans: everything owed falls due over the next 3 closes, and a close that cannot pay is a default. Saves from round 3 that are frozen keep at least two warning closes before the call, and are told the new rule once.
- **Loans.** Term loans taken in the same month are priced together (no discount for slicing one loan). Lending ARR is smoothed (the trailing mean counts for at most 1.5 × the weakest trailing month), so a one-month price spike cannot inflate capacity (never doubles it); the Bank says "lending ARR" and the HUD's run-rate is "MRR". The Bank leads with covenant status, previews covenants on draws, and its term-loan builder starts at the largest covenant-safe amount (settling round 3's open question: the full capacity breached the covenants; the reckless amount stays one edit away); quote cards suggest financing that keeps a cash cushion and considers term loans.
- **Pricing realism.** A price rise reaches today's subscribers only when its price lock ends (setPricing `grandfatherMonths`, default 6; 0 = at the next bill), with a one-off churn step by the size of the rise (half as much for grandfathered or promo subscribers); new subscribers pay the new price; cuts reach everyone at once. An optional intro promo (percent off for N months) lifts the take-rate model and ends in a smaller step. Dropping a tier moves its subscribers at the next close to the offered tier nearest in price; some cancel, more the bigger the jump. Where your take rate in a cabinet area reaches 45% on 100+ passings, the incumbent runs a promo there for 6 months ($20 off), lowering your target take rate locally and adding churn; then it stays quiet there for 6 months. Default prices are what the market bears: $60 / $75 / $95, the best constant price for a careful operator (round 4 tuning below).
- **Storms and dig-ins.** From the second year, seeded storms (their own random stream, so they never shift make-ready, signups or churn) hit an area of the map and damage aerial spans far more often than underground ones; underground plant suffers occasional dig-ins. A damaged span is out for some days: the streets it cuts off stay passed (scorecards and passings ignore outages) but their subscribers have no service, nobody signs up there, and at each close they churn more by the days they were out (each subscriber by its own days: one whose cabinet the outage cuts off is out unless another cabinet that still reaches its street has a port free for it, the service rule applied without dropping anyone; one who signed up after the repair is not; an outage over a close pays its base churn once, at the first close); while the street's own cabinet is cut off, new signups there go only to a cabinet that still reaches it with a free port; repairs are an operating cost. The covenants count repairs once, at their total over the past 12 closes, instead of annualizing a storm month from the three-month window (that turned one $7k storm into a $28k cut in the lender's cash flow and could breach a covenant on its own). The runway (HUD, briefing, advisor) takes the same view of repairs (a twelfth of the past year's) and counts the scheduled debt service (a twelfth of the year's), not a voluntary repayment, so neither a storm month nor paying down the line reads as a lasting burn and prompts advice to borrow; and once the advisor has warned that a covenant is breached or near its limit, no other suggestion in that briefing borrows, but for a covenant-safe draw that covers the payment the next close must make (ranked first), which the warning names as the only borrowing to do. About seven storms a year, each a disc of 5-15 map cells; a live aerial span in one is damaged with a 7% chance per unit of severity (1-3), an underground one 0.2%, and underground plant has 0.15 dig-ins a mile a year. An aerial span can be rebuilt underground in place (permit and construction; the aerial cable serves until the rebuild is live). Goal: neither method dominates.
- **Tuning.** The integrator's interim values (lending-ARR cap 1.5, incumbent trigger 45%, storm radius 5-15 cells) held; the round-4 tuning moved prices, the take-rate and churn weights, pole rent, the underground bore, storm frequency and damage, aerial repair costs and outage churn (see "Round 4: targets and where they landed" below).

**How the bots borrow.** SMART takes a 7-year term loan for its next cabinet project when the project is not red on its own and the projected lifetime cost per passing after it (what the bank prices on) stays at most 90% of the target (round 4: 97%, and 98% for what it builds with its own cash, since its underground plant raises it), sized to the project plus its expected connects, and only when the quote keeps a buffer (pro forma leverage ≤ 80% of the 5× limit, coverage ≥ 1.2 × the 1.2× minimum) and its reserve, which counts the next debt service and leans on the undrawn line instead of idle cash, is covered. It draws on the line when cash dips under a small floor (a month of fixed costs, the next debt service, 60 days of installs), repays when cash is above its full reserve, and repays first if a covenant is at risk. LEVERED takes every term loan the bank allows and the whole line, ignoring covenants and warnings, builds amber and red areas with heavy marketing and keeps nothing for debt service once the bank lends. The RURAL bot builds only red areas and borrows within SMART's buffers even when its reserve is short; a red-only network never covers the $9,000 overhead, so it ends in default (months 27–51), but its rate is the point.

**Why no financing value moved.** The architect's starting values met every target once SMART borrowed sensibly, so the tuning went into the bots, not the knobs. SMART ends up at leverage ~3× and coverage ~1.5× (its coverage buffer binds in later years), and the leverage band it borrows in (3–4×) sets its ~7%. LEVERED's 3.5 × ARR is ~10× leverage on the floor, so it breaches at its first close with debt and defaults a few months later. The round-2 operator sat on a $40–100k reserve for years; letting its reserve lean on the undrawn line and funding each project's expected connects with the loan took it to 54–65% of the tuning towns. Borrowing only for areas green on their own then stalled some towns once the green areas ran out (100 seeds: median 60%, 8 seeds under half), so SMART now borrows for any non-red project that keeps its lifetime cost per passing under 90% of the target (median 65%, 4 under half). Loosening the cash-flow floor or the coverage minimum would add growth too, but would also let an unviable network borrow longer while compliant (the RURAL bot is compliant until the month its cash runs out), so they stay.

Careful play passes ~35–40% of the town from its starting cash; without loans it then grows slowly (about 30% of the town at month 60, median of 100 seeds). With loans it keeps building after break-even and passes about two thirds of the town by month 60 (64%, median of 100 seeds), at ~7% and never near a covenant breach (round 4: not one warning in 100 runs, storms included).

**How the bots play (round 4).** All read the game through public sim functions only (`stormRisk`, `quoteConvert`, `quoteRemoveSpan`, `maxSafeTermLoan`, `fundingHint`, `cureOptions`, `activePromos`) and act only through commands.
- **SMART** picks each new street's method by its cost over a 12-year horizon: construction, make-ready and fees, pole rent, `stormRisk` repairs and the outage churn of the street's own subscribers (the street grid gives the rest of the plant a second path: removing any one span of a SMART network cuts off at most a few dozen subscribers), plus, for a street whose way to the cabinet is all underground, the margin it loses waiting for make-ready (30–90 days, sometimes 120–210, against a 14–45-day permit). That buries the short blocks, where one pole's rent and a repair crew's call-out cover little cable, and strings the long blocks. When a lifetime plan would break its cost-per-passing tests (what the bank prices on: it borrows while the projected lifetime CPP stays under 97% of the target and builds with its own cash under 98%), it keeps only the burials that save the most for what they cost more, then all aerial. - **PRICE_HIKER**: SMART raising every offered tier 15% every 6 closes, grandfathered for the default 6 months or passed on at the next bill.
- **The constant-price sweep** (`harness.priceSweep`): SMART with every tier at a multiple of its default price from day 0, kept there.
- **RURAL** keeps round 3's cash rules (no storm reserve), so its debt keeps buying red lanes; **MIXED** now also sets intro promos, rebuilds spans, grandfathers rises (or passes them on) and retires tiers, and the invariant sessions check price locks (one per tier, only on tiers with units), outages (only on live spans, never longer than their method's longest), the frozen-line count and the call against the stage and the debt, each close's ops figures against its stats and ledger, and the books.

**Why values moved (round 4).**
- **Prices and demand.** With the round-3 take model a careful operator earned most at about 1.3 × the defaults (500 Mbps near $70): the target take rate fell only ~2 points per 10% on every price, and nobody churned for paying more. Prices went to $60 / $75 / $95, the take model to 0.356 + 0.3 × advantage (a 55% target at the new prices against 49% before, ~3 points per 10%) and churn sensitivity from 2 to 10 (10% over the market price doubles a subscriber's churn), which puts the best constant price at the defaults on 64 of 100 towns and within 10% on 98. A steeper take slope alone would have done it, but `sales.test.ts` (rightly) wants a business at a 4× multiplier still above the minimum take rate, which caps the slope; and the cap of 0.6 had to rise to 0.7 so a faster tier at a slower tier's price still out-sells it. ARPU went from ~$68 to ~$74, still in the $60–80 band; break-even and the take rate at month 36 stayed in their bands.
- **Aerial vs underground.** Pole rent went from $2 to $7 a pole a month (4–5% of a careful operator's revenue), the bore from $28 to $26 a foot (the lowest that keeps dense underground runs in the $850–1,150 band), storms from 1 in 60 days to 1 in 50 with aerial damage 7% (was 6%) per unit of severity, an aerial repair to $1,200 + $1 a foot (was + $3: mostly the crew's call-out), outage churn to 5% + 1% a day (was 1% + 0.4%). Together: a short aerial block costs nearly as much to keep as a long one, so burying it pays within ~10 years, and the long blocks stay aerial. The storm radius stayed at the integrator's 5–15 cells: a per-span repair cost raised further made single storm months big enough to push a fully borrowed operator past its coverage covenant (it took a year of expected repairs held above the cash floor to make SMART storm-proof at these values).
- **Kept:** lending-ARR cap 1.5 (a one-month spike still cannot double borrowing), incumbent trigger 45% (5 promos a run, never everywhere), the frozen-line sweep and call (they finish every hoarder), make-ready timing (SMART's method choice already counts the wait where it matters), grandfathering, promo and retirement churn.

**What round 4 could not reach, and the sim changes that would.**
- **All-aerial out-grows the mix.** Burying a street costs about twice what stringing it does, and growth here is capital-bound and amplified by lending on revenue: every dollar saved passes more homes, which raise ARR, which the bank lends against. An all-aerial copy of SMART passes ~6 points more of the town by month 60 and is worth more at month 60 (cash − debt + 12 years of operating cash flow: more on 73 of 100); in a 20-seed probe it was still ahead after 10 years; SMART's 12-year cost of owning its plant per passing is about equal ($1,205 vs $1,214, all-underground $1,020). No storm, rent or churn level tried made the mix win on growth without crippling every aerial operator (repairs past ~8% of revenue froze and defaulted careful players on bad storm years). Making the mix genuinely better would need the sim to give underground an edge where it is built, e.g. make-ready cost drawn per street (some blocks need pole replacements costing more than a bore), storm exposure that varies by area, or aerial plant that wears out.
- **Subscribers are only modestly less exposed.** A storm cuts off only the damaged block, and SMART buries the short blocks, where few subscribers live; the long residential blocks are aerial in both strategies, so all-aerial's subscribers are only 10–33% more exposed (expected outage days and cancellations), not twice.
- **Storm sequences diverge between strategies.** Each storm draws a damage roll for every span in its area from the weather stream, so two plants on one seed soon see different storms; realized storm losses compare luck as much as plant on a handful of seeds. The round-4 target therefore checks expected exposure per seed and quotes realized totals on 100 seeds. Drawing each storm's damage from its own sub-stream (weather.ts) would make the realized comparison fair seed by seed.
- **Rebuilds rarely pay.** A rebuild pays the whole bore and $2,500 of fees to save a span's pole rent, repairs and outage churn (~$150–250 a year), so SMART rebuilt one span in 100 runs and the advisor's rebuild suggestion stays rare.
- **Churn's price reference is `TIER_DEFAULTS`** (sales.ts): a player who sets prices is compared with the defaults, not with what the market would bear at the moment; decoupling them would need a sales.ts change.

### Why values moved (round 4b)

- **The incumbent.** Its trigger went from 45% to 50%. SMART's areas settle at ~41–46% (the median area at month 60), so at 45% a mature area triggered promos as a matter of course; at 50% only an area doing unusually well does (an apartment block, a dense corner, or prices pushed down: AGGRESSIVE meets one or two). Its extra churn went from 1% to 3% a month: at 1% the promo cost an area about 6% of its subscribers, and no answer beat ignoring it by more than the game's own randomness (two runs that differ by a few hundred dollars of cash drift ±$5–10k over 18 months). At 3% ignoring it costs about a sixth of the area's subscribers. The retention offer went from 15% to 12% off, and its end-of-lock churn share from 0.25 to 1 (the discount ends like a rise passed on at the next bill). So it pays over a promo's months (it keeps about half the cancellations a promo causes, for less than they are worth), but not with only a close or two left (the step at its end costs more than a month saves). The pricing tests (`incumbent.test.ts`) need exactly that.
- **Storm exposure.** Industrial streets went from 0.9 to 0.7 (yards and low sheds, few trees near the lines). The band thresholds went from 0.85 / 1.3 to 1 / 1.2: a plain residential street is exactly 1 (moderate), so downtown and industrial blocks are sheltered and residential streets with trees or near the edge are exposed. At the integrator's values the sheltered band was 7–12% of the tuning towns and the moderate one 70–78%. The storm-center weight went from 1 to 1.25: storms lean a little more toward exposed ground than the town, which keeps all-aerial's expected repairs at 1.3× SMART's or more on every tuning seed once industrial streets became sheltered.
- **SMART's storm weight** (2, over 13 years) is the bot side of the same balance. With storms weighed as expected, the exposure change left SMART burying under 20% on one tuning seed (2027) and all-aerial's repairs under 1.25× SMART's on another (424242).
- **Kept:** the demand segments (the integrator's: they keep the default lineup's revenue per household within ~1.5% of round 4's), the retrigger points (5), the retention churn cut (1), the intro promo's signup boost (2), horizon (24 months) and quote horizon (12 months), the snooze and expand values, the storm frequency, damage, repair and outage values, the wooded and edge terms, and every financing value. No bank value moved: loosening the floor margin, the credit-line multiple or the ARR spike cap brought Bootstrapped to 4–5 of 5 only with all three together, and each lets an unviable network borrow longer (round 3's reason for keeping them).

### What round 4b could not reach, and the sim changes that would

- **Shoestring is not winnable** (0 of 5, 0 of 100). The overhead is a fixed $9,000 a month. A careful network covers it from about 300 mature passings (about $250,000 of plant) plus the installs and the burn of a year's ramp. $200,000 buys about 190 passings and leaves almost nothing for installs. The bank lends nothing before revenue and then about 1.2–1.5× ARR (the covenants on the 35%-of-ARR floor), so every lean, bridged or promo-driven plan tried (several dozen SMART settings, with and without looser bank values) defaults within a year or never breaks even. With a $3,000 overhead the lean bot wins on 4 of 5 (at $4,000 on 2–3). Bootstrapped survives at $9,000 only thinly: 5 of 5 tuning seeds, but 31 of 100 towns default, 19 of them in a cash crunch in months 7–12 before the bank lends enough, the rest after years of near-zero cash flow (months 17–57). At $8,000 a lean bot survived all five with a median break-even at month 17. **Sim change:** overhead by level (the "more knobs later" the owner anticipated: about $8,000 Bootstrapped and $3,000–4,000 Shoestring), or an overhead that grows with the network (a base plus a share per passing, re-tuned so the Seed round keeps its break-even).
- **SMART's mix does not beat all-aerial on 5-year value** (1 of 5, 15 of 100). Growth is capital-bound and lent against revenue: a dollar not spent on a bore passes more homes, which earn about 30% a year and raise the ARR the bank lends against. In runValue (cash − debt + 12 years of operating cash flow) a dollar spent up front costs about four, so burying pays only where a street's yearly aerial cost (rent, repairs, outage churn) reaches about a third of the bore's extra cost. The exposure model's variation sits at the map's edges and in groves, outside the dense areas both operators build: 97% of SMART's plant is unwooded and over 10 cells from the edge, at exposure 1 (residential) or 0.6 (downtown). So all-aerial strings nothing much riskier than what SMART buries. A SMART that buries only when the bore pays back four times over its horizon ties all-aerial (it becomes all-aerial), and no storm level with repairs under ~8% of revenue gave the mix an edge (a static search over 800 combinations of zone, wooded, edge, center-weight, damage, frequency, radius, repair and outage-churn values). **Sim change:** exposure that varies inside the town (tree-lined residential blocks drawn independently of the lots, or wind fetch by street orientation), aerial plant that wears (repairs rising with age), or make-ready drawn per street (some blocks need pole replacements that cost more than a bore).
- **The intro promo pays in a mature town too** (the quote pays and the realized run wins on 5 of 5). The signup boost speeds every street's gap toward its target alike, and churn keeps a mature town's gap about as large per signup as a fresh area's: in the take model, the extra subscriber-months per discounted signup differ by only the churn-to-ramp ratio (~15%) between the two. PROMO_SIGNUP_BOOST from 0.25 to 2 moves both together (at 0.5 neither pays). **Sim change:** a boost that acts on young streets only (lit within the last months, or under half their target), or a discount that also reaches the existing subscribers who would otherwise churn, so a mature town pays it on everyone.
- **Fragile kept targets.** Several round-2..4 targets sit near their edge on one tuning seed: seed 99's share at month 60 is 51.1% (≥ 50%), seed 1's lifetime cost per passing at month 60 is $990 (≤ $1,000), seed 424242's all-aerial repairs are 1.30× (≥ 1.25×). - **The no-loan SMART gap** (≥ 15 points, kept) holds at 26–28 points.
- **Following the advisor at the other levels** (review of round 4b; targets4b now runs the follower at Angel-backed and Bootstrapped too). At **Angel-backed** the follower never goes bust and is worth more than SMART-lite at month 60 on 4 of 5 (77 of 100), but passes less of the town on most (59–80% against SMART-lite's 70–75%; 21 of 100 pass more and are worth more): SMART-lite spends the whole purse on day one, while the advisor suggests one build at a time, each keeping its reserve. The noisy comparisons (support, switching, the incumbent's promo answers, the intro promo in a fresh and a mature town) average over 3 replicates whose sales stream is re-seeded (`replicateState`, a test-only state edit; the weather stream is kept, so every replicate meets the same storms): common random numbers. Two runs that differ by one decision drift apart through who signs up and who cancels when, by ±$10,000 over a year or three, more than most levers are worth; the replicates measure a lever's expected worth in that situation. The switching experiment measures "reaching the ceiling" against the ceiling at the default prices (spec I.1's realistic ceiling, which a better deal reaches sooner): churn keeps every lineup's areas below their own target, where switching into the gap balances the cancellations out of it, so the default lineup settles at 85–90% of its own ceiling and never "reaches" it by its own measure.

### Why values moved (round 4c/4d)

The workstreams (sim-economy, sim-customers) set the first values; two tuning passes moved them (the first left nine targets failing, the second fixed them). Each value's comment in `balance.ts` says why. In short:

- **The scaled overhead.** Base $2,000 → $2,500 and per customer $2 → $8 → $9 (first and second pass), per cabinet $200 → $250 and per route-mile $100 → $125 (second pass). A customer's running cost is what makes a cheaper lineup a real trade-off (at $2 a lineup 17% below the defaults earned nearly as much per customer and signed many more), and the per-plant lines make a network cost more to run as it grows: the Seed round's careful operator borrows against its cash flow and passed 67% of the town at month 60; now 61%, while Bootstrapped and Shoestring, whose networks are small, are barely touched. A larger base (tried $3,000–3,500) brought the break-even no later but killed Shoestring (2 of 5 winnable).
- **Switching and happiness** (first pass). Switching 1/450 → 1/525 a day (with happy customers' word of mouth streets filled too fast); happy from a score of −0.2 instead of 0.2 (at 0.2 nobody was happy on Good support at the default prices, so word of mouth and referral credits did nothing); the happiest customers churn at 0.7 → 0.8 of the base (second pass: Great support earns its keep by lifting customers out of an outage's unhappiness, not by trimming the churn of customers with nothing to complain about); outages remembered 90 → 180 days; a good deal's happiness 2 → 0.25 per share under the market (at 2 a cheap lineup dominated); marketing's default $1,000 → $500.
- **Support levels.** Great $7 / +0.3 → $6 / +0.6 (first pass: at +0.3 it never lifted anyone out of an outage); Basic −0.4 → −0.6 (second pass: at −0.4 its customers were merely okay and the $2 it saves outweighed the few extra cancellations; now they are unhappy and Basic loses customers and money on every tuning seed).
- **Campaigns** (first pass): $12 → $20 per unsigned unit and the switching boost 0.5 → 1, so a campaign pays in a freshly lit area (many unaware homes) and not in a well-signed one.
- **Price steps** (second pass). The churn per share of a rise 0.4 → 0.8, grandfathered steps 0.5 → 0.75 of it, the intro promo's step 0.5 → 0.25 and the retention offer's 1 → 0.5 (so those two end exactly as before). With happiness a price hiker's customers leave month by month for paying over the market, so its network was gone before many of them met a price step (round 4's PRICE_HIKER target counts the step cancellations).
- **The incumbent** (second pass). Its promo churn 3% → 5% a month (spec C: answering must matter; at 3% the best answer beat ignoring on two of five towns), and the retrigger step 0.05 → 0.07 (first pass) → 0.1, since a promo now knocks an area's take further down and an aggressive operator's area climbed back past the old step to a third promo.
- **The intro promo.** Its boost 2 → 2.5 (first pass: it still pays in a fresh area against the slower switching) and its young-street window 12 → 6 months (second pass: SMART lights streets until its last year, so a "mature" town at month 48 still held enough young streets for a standing promo to pay).
- **Storm economics** (first pass, spec D). Aerial damage chance 0.07 → 0.04 with the canopy factor 6 → 8 (damage moves onto tree-lined streets), storms 1/50 → 1/55 a day, canopy repairs 1 → 2 × the street's canopy extra, underground repairs $4,000 → $3,000 base, and the method choice's up-front weight 3 → 1.5 (buries tree-lined streets that pay back within 8 years, keeps open blocks aerial).
- **The advisor's covenant headroom** (second pass): a financed build (and the runway and purchase financing) must leave the covenants 25% inside their limits, not 15%. With the scaled overhead a thin network's cash flow falls as new plant comes on before the new area pays, and a second financed build two months after the first was priced on a trailing year that did not yet show the first one's costs: at 15% the Bootstrapped follower breached its coverage covenant and defaulted on seed 12345.
- **Kept:** the difficulty purses, the bank (rates, capacity, covenants, the floor margin), cable and cabinet costs, the demand segments and tier prices, the take-rate model, the referral credit ($25) and word-of-mouth multiplier, the campaign's length, cooldown and minimum cost, the storm radius and severity, and every round-4b advisor value but the headroom.
- **Test and harness changes.** The switching experiment measures "reaching the ceiling" against the ceiling at the default prices (spec I.1's realistic ceiling): churn keeps every lineup's areas below their own target, so the default lineup never reached 90% of its own and the measure was undefined for it. The lever, switching, promo and incumbent experiments average three replicates (above). `sales.test.ts` and `pricingPanel.test.ts` now build the promo advice's wording from PROMO_BOOST_YOUNG_MONTHS; `incumbent.test.ts`'s intro-promo answer uses a one-subscriber area (with a 5% promo churn the retention offer was the larger remedy for ten); `review4.test.ts` gives the called hoarder the cash repaying everything needs (its first called close now holds barely more than it owes).

### What round 4c/4d could not reach, and the sim changes that would

- **The Seed round's break-even sits at the low edge of 12–15** (tuning median 12; 11 on 100 seeds). The first positive month comes when a network's revenue passes its running costs, and the Seed round's first build passes ~700 homes in the first months; no overhead level moved it later without breaking a smaller level first: a larger base (+$500 to +$1,000 a month) did not move the median at all and made Shoestring unwinnable; slower switching (1/650–1/700 a day) moved it to 13–14 but made a cheaper lineup the best constant price and Bootstrapped's share fall under 35%; slower awareness (on lighting, base, marketing) moved nothing. **Sim change:** a ramp cost that only a large first build feels (installs or marketing per newly lit home in its first months), or a first-build cap at the Seed round. The round-4c/4d review left it there (review bug 10, low): it is a month inside the tuning band, and every retune tried moved a working target instead.
- **Great support pays under high exposure on only 45 of 100 towns** (5 of 5 tuning seeds; the review made its quote look three years ahead with the outages the plant can expect, so the advisor now suggests it where storms keep knocking customers out, but the realized gap is unchanged): its extra $2 a customer and the churn it saves are close to even on most storm-exposed networks over three years (the median difference is −$1,300), and the comparison that keeps it from paying over a calm year (95 of 100) pulls the other way. **Balance or sim change:** an outage that weighs more on happiness (HAPPINESS_PER_OUTAGE_DAY) would widen the gap where storms are frequent; it also raises every storm-exposed network's churn, so it needs its own tuning pass.
- **A premium beyond ×1.05 still costs most of its value.** The review let customers pay up to 8% over the market without complaint (`HAPPINESS_MARKET_PREMIUM_TOLERATED`; at 0, 5% over cost 31–40% of five-year value): ×1.05 is now within 11% of the defaults and worth more on two of five tuning towns, but ×1.1 earns $239,000 of five-year operating cash flow against $546,000 at ×1.0 (round 4c/4d: $191,000), mostly through the take-rate model's price response (the sweep's take falls from 40% to 30%). **Balance change:** a gentler price response above the defaults in `segmentDemand`, which moves every price target with it.
- **SMART's mix against all-underground** (kept: ahead on every tuning seed, 5 of 5) is ahead on only 70 of 100 towns (round 4b: 96), and against all-aerial on 86 (round 4b: 15). SMART's storm cushion (a year of its plant's expected repairs off its cash flow when it borrows) costs a storm-exposed aerial network borrowing room that a buried one does not need, and on heavily tree-lined towns burying nearly everything is close to optimal: SMART leads all-underground by $21,000–196,000 on seed 1 depending on small changes. Without the cushion SMART breached its covenants after big storms on some towns (the round-3 target "never past a warning"). **Sim change:** covenants that smooth a storm's repairs over more than a trailing year, or storm damage spread over more, smaller storms (the same expected repairs with less variance).
- **SMART's mix against all-underground at Shoestring** (review, spec D at the small purses). The review's lean planner put SMART ahead at Bootstrapped (all-aerial 88 of 100, all-underground 66, mean $1.24M against $0.81M and $1.12M) and ahead of all-aerial at Shoestring (68 of 100), but at Shoestring burying everything is still worth more on 60 of 100 towns (mean $279,000 against $252,000; game overs 5 against 10). On a $200,000 purse the first area is the company: all underground it lights with no make-ready wait (a long one is 120–210 days on 15% of aerial orders) and owes no storm repairs, which a thin company cannot carry; tried and dropped: weighing storms twice, the feeder's wait over the whole area, a higher wait weight. **Bot or sim change:** a lean planner that prices the whole first build both ways by its time to first revenue and the cash its worst storm year needs, or storm damage spread over more, smaller storms (the same expected repairs with less variance).
- **SMART's underground share** (20–60% every seed, kept) is 21.4–21.5% on the least exposed towns (seed 2027 among the tuning seeds), whose dense cores have few tree-lined streets: spec D buries by exposure, so a sheltered town buries less, and the band's floor is close.

### Round 4c/4d review fixes

The round-4c/4d review found 22 bugs. Each fix has a test that failed before it and is kept. Balance values moved only where a bug was an unmet spec target (bug 14's `HAPPINESS_MARKET_PREMIUM_TOLERATED`; `SUPPORT_QUOTE_MONTHS` is a new quote horizon), the careful operator's settings for bugs 8 and 9, and every target was re-checked on the tuning seeds and on 100 seeds (the round-4c/4d tables above are the re-measured numbers). Bug 10, the Seed round's break-even at the band's low edge, is left as is (see "What round 4c/4d could not reach").

**The customers model.**
- **The intro promo in a mature town (bug 1).** The promo lifted the take rate and the deal everywhere, so a standing promo paid in a well-signed town. Now it is valued only on young or contested streets (`customers.promoApplies`); elsewhere it only discounts its signups. `quotePromo` looks `promoQuoteMonths` ahead (the promo's months plus a year), counts unit-months against the margin, the installs that come sooner rather than extra, and the splitters, and its "pays now" advice no longer says "mature town". In a mature town the quote never pays and the promo loses on at least 4 of 5 seeds for each of four offers, by operating cash flow and by cash with installs (`MATURE_PROMO_CASES`).
- **"Not worth it" counted homes still deciding (bugs 3, 20).** The reasons now have a "still deciding" row (aware homes who have not switched yet at a fair deal), so "not worth it" counts only homes priced out.
- **The Areas list (bug 4)** counts each account under the cabinet that serves its street today (`service.servingCabinet`), as the sales model does.
- **Migrated saves (bug 5).** A save from before the customers model came in with no street records, so every lit street looked freshly lit (promo boost, awareness at its start). `customers.seedStreets` dates each lit street from when it went live (`network.litSinceDays`) or its first customer, with the awareness it would have grown since.
- **Forecasts capped at the free ports (bug 11).** `forecastCustomers` lets signups take the ports splitters would add (up to `MAX_SPLITTERS_PER_CABINET`) and reports the splitters it needs; every customers quote (marketing, campaign, support, referrals) and the advisor's growth options now count their cost. A campaign in a freshly lit area now quotes its signups instead of almost none.
- **A campaign boosted streets lit after it was bought (bug 12).** It was priced on the area's lit homes but boosted every street of the area for its months. Now it boosts only the streets lit when it starts (`campaignStrengthOn`).
- **Great support was never quoted as paying (bug 13).** `quoteSupport` looks `SUPPORT_QUOTE_MONTHS` (36) ahead with the outages the network's plant can expect (each street's storm risk along its path from its cabinet), and the advisor's card says "over the next three years with the outages your cable can expect". On an all-aerial network it suggests Great support where storms keep knocking customers out.
- **A price above the defaults collapsed (bug 14).** Every share over the market's price made customers unhappy, so 5% over cost a third of five-year value. `HAPPINESS_MARKET_PREMIUM_TOLERATED` (8%) is the premium customers pay without complaint; past it the happiness factor applies as before (an overpriced lineup at x1.2 still makes them unhappy, and grows clearly slower, but no longer defaults). Every price x1.05 is now worth 0.89–1.05 of the defaults at month 60 on the tuning towns.

**The advisor.**
- **No first build at Shoestring (bugs 2, 7).** Where no area the purse covers pays its running costs once signed up, the card now offers the build that cuts the monthly loss the most (`shrinksBurn`), sized to the cash, instead of "pick a dense street" with no command; the follower builds on Shoestring seed 12345 and does at least as well as SMART-lite there.
- **A second cabinet in the first week (bug 6).** expand waits until the company's first area lights; the Seed round's seed 13 follower no longer runs out of cash.
- **Growth levers into a covenant breach (found by the re-check).** A campaign's price comes off the cash flow the covenants measure (the trailing three closes, annualized: four times for a quarter), so right after a financed build it breached on the Bootstrapped seed 1 follower. With debt, a growth lever now must leave the covenants `EXPAND_COVENANT_HEADROOM` inside their limits after its spend, as a loan must.
- **Plain words (bugs 18, 19):** no covenant jargon in the summary's next step ("Pay back all $X you owe", "Raise profit to about $X a year", the Bank's word for yearly bills less running costs), and the frozen line's card says "Get back to borrowing within N months". The checker's pass found the same jargon behind "More about this" on the payment, runway and build cards ("within the covenants", "No covenant-safe borrowing covers it", "leaves the covenants 25% inside their limits"): they now say "inside the bank's limits", and the advisor tests check every card's detail for bare bank terms (leverage, covenant, ×, bps) as well as its first view.

**The careful operator (bugs 8, 9).**
- **SMART's mix at the small purses (bug 8).** The lean planner ranked areas and streets at the cheapest method's prices and then built them street by street with the lifetime method choice, burying the exposed half; a small purse, which buys only part of an area, bought sparse areas, and burying everything matched or beat it at Bootstrapped and Shoestring. `LEAN_OPTIONS` now prices what it builds (`chosenCosts`), plans each candidate area and builds the one with the lowest cost per passing (`bestOfPlans`), and weighs a street's make-ready wait at three times its lost margin (`waitWeight`). All-aerial and all-underground share the planner (they are SMART with the method forced). At the Seed round's purse the first two measured slightly better over 25 towns but lost tuning seed 1 to all-underground, so they stay lean settings. Over 100 towns SMART's mix now leads at Bootstrapped (ahead of all-aerial on 88, all-underground on 66) and leads all-aerial at Shoestring (68), Shoestring's defaults fell from 16 to 10 and Bootstrapped's median share rose from 38% to 43%; at Shoestring all-underground is still ahead (see "What round 4c/4d could not reach").
- **Seed 91 (bug 9).** A severe storm took the Seed-round SMART's cash below zero with its line drawn, and it missed the next payment. SMART now bridges with a term loan at every purse (`bridgeMonths` 3, round 4b's lean setting): seed 91 survives, and no Seed-round SMART defaults on 100 seeds.

**The screens (bugs 15–17, 21, 22).** The desktop HUD wraps its stats instead of overflowing; the summary's headline takes its tone color (a CSS specificity fix); the phone's money-in label falls back to a legend when its segment is too narrow; the marketing forecast says there is nothing to forecast with nothing lit; the referral line no longer contradicts itself ("hardly any of them extra"), and (the checker's pass) neither does the marketing line: with most homes already aware it said "about 3 new customers a month, about $23,360 per new customer it brings"; under the count's floor it now says marketing adds hardly any of them.

**Test and harness changes.** The incumbent-answer experiment picks a promo the game has not already answered (answering an answered promo played exactly like ignoring it); the campaign experiment averages three replicates like the other noisy comparisons; the UI purity fixture runs its campaign once the area's streets are all lit (a campaign now boosts only those).
